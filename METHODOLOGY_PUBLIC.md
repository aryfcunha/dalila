# Methodology

Dalila is built to identify and surface news that matters to the UAE’s humanitarian and development agenda. Our process combines real-time data collection with rigorous filtering to ensure every brief is both relevant and actionable.

## Data Collection
We monitor a curated set of global news wires, state press agencies, and multilateral development feeds. By polling these sources every 30 minutes, we ensure our coverage is timely without sacrificing the depth needed for professional analysis.

## Intelligent Filtering
Not all news is development news. Every item passes through a multi-stage filter that evaluates it against a specific UAE-focused lens. We prioritize institutional moves, policy shifts, and significant humanitarian developments, while automatically removing noise and duplicates across different outlets.

## Categorization and Scoring
Each selected item is categorized into one of eight sectors (e.g., Humanitarian, UAE Foreign Policy, Reports & Evidence) and scored for its relevance to the UAE mission. This allows the daily brief to be organized logically, helping readers quickly find the most impactful stories in their specific areas of interest.

## Early Warning and Foresight
Beyond traditional news, Dalila tracks emerging trends through conflict-risk indices and prediction markets. We use a "change-only" rule: we only report on a situation when there is a measurable shift from the baseline, ensuring that our Foresight section provides true early warning rather than repeating known states.

## Transparency
While the pipeline is automated to ensure consistency and speed, the rules it follows are documented and stable. This methodology ensures that every brief is reproducible and grounded in a clearly defined editorial framework focused on UAE development priorities.

## Strategic Foresight Methodology
Dalila uses **Log-Odds Shift** scoring to identify the most significant market movements. This mathematical approach prioritises events where the crowd is rapidly changing its mind—particularly in low-probability "tail risks" where a shift from 1% to 10% reflects a massive increase in underlying risk.

Our discovery engine weights different topics by their relevance to the current news cycle and its alignment with UAE, humanitarian aid, and development.
